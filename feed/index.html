]]> The Solar Eclipse of 2024 is an exceedingly rare event that occurs when the moon’s dark inner shadow intersects Earth along a thin track, famously known as the path of totality. This fascinating phenomenon will grace Hochatown/Broken Bow in Southeast Oklahoma, placing it right in the heart of the total solar eclipse. Missing out on this awe-inspiring event would be a great regret, so mark your calendars and prepare for an unforgettable experience!  

Tips to consider while preparing for the Solar Eclipse of 2024: 

 1. Research the Eclipse: Learn about the date, time, and location of the eclipse you plan to observe. Understand the path of totality and how it will pass through different areas. 

2. Time, Location, & Duration of Totality: In the Hochatown/ Broken Bow area, the solar eclipse is scheduled to take place on Monday, April 8, 2024. It promises a series of captivating moments.  Learn about the stages of a total solar eclipse, including the partial phases leading up to totality. Familiarize yourself with the timing and duration of each phase so you can anticipate and fully enjoy the experience. 

Hochatown/Broken Bow , Ok 

Monday, April 8, 2024 12;30 pm-3 pm

Partial Begins at 12:28 pm

Totality Begins at 1:45 pm

Totality Ends at 1:49 pm

Partial Ends at 3:06 pm

 3. Travel and Accommodations: Plan your travel and accommodations well in advance. Popular viewing areas may get crowded, so booking early is recommended. Be aware of traffic conditions and allow for extra travel time. In preparation for this extraordinary celestial display, Bear Mountain Lodging is pleased to provide exclusive cabin lodging options in close proximity to the viewing area. Our accommodations range from cozy 1-bedroom cabins to spacious 5-bedroom retreats, accommodating groups of 2-18 guests.  Visit our website to learn more about each cabin and our availability- www.visitbrokenbowcabins.com

4. Safety Glasses: To safeguard your eyes during this event, certified solar eclipse glasses or solar filters are a must have. Regular sunglasses or homemade filters are not sufficient to protect your eyes. Ensure that the glasses you use meet the proper safety standards. Remember, unless during totality, direct exposure to the sun can lead to permanent eye damage, making these glasses an essential accessory.  As a bonus, if you plan to be a guest with Bear Mountain Lodging, we prioritize your safety and enjoyment by supplying ISO compliant solar eclipse eyeglasses, available in your cabin prior to your arrival.  

5. Snacks and Supplies: Bring snacks, water, and any necessary supplies with you  to ensure your comfort during the eclipse. It’s a good idea to pack sunscreen, insect repellent, and appropriate clothing for the weather conditions. 

6. Camera and Equipment: If you’re interested in capturing photographs or videos of the eclipse, prepare your camera and equipment in advance. Use appropriate solar filters for your camera lenses to avoid damage from direct sunlight.  

7. Observation Tools: Consider bringing binoculars or a small telescope to enhance your viewing experience. These can provide a closer look at the details of the sun during the partial phases, but remember to use appropriate solar filters. 

8. Enjoy the Moment: Finally, make sure to take a moment to fully immerse yourself in the experience. Put your devices aside, take a deep breath, and enjoy the majesty of the total solar eclipse. It’s a rare phenomenon that deserves your full attention.

The booming tourism area in Hochatown has just won its right to be known as its own town!  The small town of  Hochatown, with a population of around 250, is only 8 miles from Broken Bow, but this cabin area had always been referred to as “Broken Bow Cabins” which ended up confusing some tourists. After a long battle for the locals,  the votes were tallied and on November 8, 2022, it became official!  

The original Hochatown was founded by the Choctaw Indians. The Choctaw tribe originally settled on the site of what is now Hochatown in the 1880s, with just 12 families moving into the area. Hochatown flourished thanks to the presence of the Choctaw Lumber and Coal Company (later renamed Dierks). To promote log export, the lumber company constructed a railroad spur connecting Hochatown and Eagletown, Oklahoma. During the 1920s and 1930s, prime lumber supply dwindled and the lumber company moved to other local areas. In the meantime, Hochatown developed from a rowdy lumber camp into Oklahoma’s “Moonshine Capital.”  The town’s glory days quickly ended, and the final family left in 1966 after the post office closed in 1963 in preparation of the dam being built.  While all other structures were demolished, the town church and cemetery were relocated to higher land. The area is now submerged by 200+ feet of water.

The second incarnation of Hochatown is located approximately one mile west of Broken Bow Lake on US Route 259 with an economy that thrives through tourism. In addition to Broken Bow Lake, the area includes Beavers Bend Resort Park, Hochatown State Park, and Cedar Creek Golf Course. Hunters also visit the region, which bills itself as the “deer capital of the world.

The area is now home to couples’ cabins suitable for romantic getaways and family cabins to accommodate larger groups. There are also lots of things to do in Hochatown with new restaurants and attractions being added all the time! You can experience nature at its best with outdoor activities that include hiking, boating, fishing, kayaking, and much more! Make plans to visit the area to see what all it has to offer!
